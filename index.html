01 / 03 — The demand side

A student gets one shot at this decision. Most take it blind.

College choice in India is made on hearsay, a cousin's opinion, and whichever agent reached the family first. There is no neutral map. The cost of a wrong seat is four years and a career.

02 / 03 — The supply side

Institutions burn budget on unqualified footfall.

Colleges spend heavily to fill seats and still finish the cycle short. Their acquisition is untracked, unattributed, and impossible to forecast. They are buying noise, not enrolment.

03 / 03 — The middle

The consultancy layer is fragmented and unaccountable.

Thousands of single-desk agents, no data, no standards, no follow-through after the commission clears. Nobody owns the student after admission — which is exactly where the lifetime value sits.

Elevation 05 · Defensibility

Four things a competitor cannot buy with a funding round.

M1

Supply-side lock-in

500+ institutional relationships built over four years, contract by contract. A new entrant starts at zero inventory and every partner already has a signed counterpart.

M2

Geographic density in Kerala

10+ sub-offices in a single high-intent state. Density beats spread in this category — it produces referral compounding that national ad spend cannot replicate.

M3

The admissions dataset

Five thousand closed admissions with outcome data. Every cycle sharpens the AI career map. The recommendation engine gets better while competitors are still collecting rows.

M4

Trust, held by people

Families do not hand a child's future to a website. Trained counsellors carry the brand into the living room — a distribution asset that takes years, not capital, to build.
